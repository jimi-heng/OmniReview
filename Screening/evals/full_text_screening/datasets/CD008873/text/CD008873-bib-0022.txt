Introduction Atopy is defined as a tendency to become sensitised and produce IgE antibodies in response to ordinary exposure to allergens, usually proteins (1) . Early life events, including those before birth, influence the development of atopy. Those at the atopic response extreme already have elevated specific IgE at birth (2) . T-helper lymphocytes (Th) play a central role in atopic development (3, 4) . Th lymphocytes differentiate into subgroups in response to the maturity of the cells presenting antigens to them and the cytokines to which they are exposed (5) . Th2 lymphocytes are the Th subset principally involved in the development of atopy (6) . Vitamin D receptors are present on many immune cell types, with vitamin D having effects on cells within both the innate and adaptive components of the immune system. The biologically active form of vitamin D, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25-(OH) 2 7 D), causes macrophages and monocytes to proliferate and modulates their cytokine production ( ). Under the influence of 1,25-(OH) 2 D dendritic cells reduce expression of costimulatory molecules, with inhibition of interleukin 12induced Th1 lymphocyte skewing. In the presence of 1,25-(OH) 2 5 D, dendritic cells become more tolerogenic, and promote preferential expansion of regulatory T lymphocytes ( , 8). Regulatory T lymphocytes decrease production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interferon gamma, and increase production of immune-modulatory cytokines interleukin 10 and transforming growth factor beta (8) . The relationship between vitamin D 9 status and atopy is non-linear ( ). We recently completed a randomised, doubleblind, placebo-controlled parallel-group trial of vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy and infancy in New Zealand (NZ) (11) . In NZ there is a sun-avoidance public health policy, the diet has a low vitamin D content and vitamin D 12 supplementation is used infrequently ( , 13). Hence vitamin D deficiency is prevalent with 57% of newborns having cord blood 25(OH)D 14 concentrations <50 nmol/L ( ). Both asthma and atopy are highly prevalent in NZ with 25% of six-to-seven year olds having reported asthma (15) , and 35% of 11-to-12 year olds being atopic (16) . All rights reservedMethodsTrial design We conducted a previously described randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study in Auckland, NZ (latitude 36°S) (11, 17) . The objective of this study was to determine the vitamin D dose necessary to achieve 25(OH)D concentration ≥ 50 nmol/L during infancy (11) . We randomly assigned pregnant mothers (from enrolment at 27 weeks gestation to birth) and their infants (from birth to age 6 months), to one of three groups: daily placebo; lower-dose; or higherdose oral vitamin D (Figure 1 ). Ethical approval was from the Ministry of Health ethics committee (NTX09/11/101). We obtained written informed consent from all participating women. Registration was with the Australian NZ Clinical Trials Registry (ACTRN12610000483055).Participants We recruited women from an urban primary care maternity clinic from April 2010 to July 2011. All rights reservedData collection We interviewed enrolled women during pregnancy and when their infant was 2, 4, 6, and 18 months old. For this study, we collected data describing demographics, adherence, mother's food consumption, infant feeding, sunlight exposure, primary care visits for asthma, and drugs prescribed (11, 17) . At the assessment completed when the children were 18 months old, we obtained a capillary blood sample from each child and measured serum 25(OH)D concentration (11, 17) . We determined aeroallergen sensitisation by skin prick testing (SPT) to house dust mite, cat and grass pollens (Supplementary Table 1 ) (19) ; and semi-quantitative measurement (ImmunoCAP, Pharmacia, Uppsala, Sweden) of specific serum IgE antibodies to antigens in the following groups: animal; polcalcin; grass, tree, and weed pollens; mould; mites; and cockroach (Supplementary Table 1 ) (20) . We defined a positive SPT response as a wheal diameter ≥3mm larger than the negative control (21) . We categorised IgE responses as undetectable (ISAC Standardized Units (ISU) 0-0•3), low (ISU >0•3-1•0), moderate/high (ISU >1-15) and very high (ISU >15). We reviewed the primary care records for the doctor diagnosis assigned at each acute respiratory illness visit. In NZ each patient's primary care file includes a free text record completed by the doctor at each visit and stored in an electronic practice management system. Each patient's records are identified by their National Health Index number, a unique identifier assigned to each person in NZ at their first contact with the healthcare system. We read each child's primary care record and assigned each respiratory illness visit to one of nine diagnostic categories based upon the doctor's written record. These categories were a cold, otitis media, an upper respiratory infection, croup, asthma, bronchitis, bronchiolitis, a wheezy lower respiratory infection or fever and cough. We described maternal and child characteristics, including those specifically relevant to vitamin D status. We compared maternal and child characteristics between study groups and also between groups based upon whether they had serum obtained at age 18 months. We used the chi-square and Fisher's exact tests to compare proportions, and the Wilcoxon rank sum test to compare continuous data. For the comparison of the semi-quantitative specific IgE responses, we combined the moderate/high and very high categories. All rights reservedDiscussionStatement of principal findings In this post-hoc analysis of a randomised, placebo-controlled trial of vitamin D supplementation during the third trimester of pregnancy and the first six months of infancy we showed that vitamin D supplementation resulted in a decrease in the proportion of children sensitised to HDM at age 18 months. The number of mite antigens to which the children were sensitised and the magnitude of IgE responses to these mite antigens were both lower in the vitamin D supplemented groups. In comparison with the placebo group the proportion of children with any primary care visits for which asthma was recorded as being present and the number of such primary care asthma visits per child were smaller in the vitamin D supplemented groups; as was the proportion of these primary care asthma visits and the number of visits per child for which salbutamol or prednisone were prescribed.Strengths and weaknesses of the study Vitamin D was given from 27 weeks gestation and continued until the infants were six months old. Atopic sensitisation can start in utero (2) . Children of atopic parents who have evidence at birth of sensitisation to mites are at increased risk of developing allergic symptoms by age one year (22) . Interventions that seek to prevent atopy sensitisation are therefore likely to need to begin before birth. As immune responses to allergens can be detected as early as 22 weeks gestation, vitamin D supplementation may have been more effective if started earlier in pregnancy (23) . We used a daily dose of vitamin D and thus avoided the wide variations in serum 25(OH)D concentration seen when large intermittent vitamin D doses are used and which are believed to cause immune responses that are not beneficial to the host (24, 25) . We maintained study blinding until the children were 18 months old and at this age measured atopic sensitisation and acute primary care visits. The electronic primary care records we audited to describe primary care asthma and other respiratory illness visits were not coded. Hence they were not subject to the under-estimations, by approximately 25%, of primary care asthma visits in preschool aged children that result when only coded primary care data are used (26) . As the description of primary care visits was not one of the primary objectives of the trial, we would expect the care received by the children in this study to reflect usual practice. There is considerable variability between doctors in their assignment of specific diagnoses to episodes of acute respiratory illness in young children (27) . Strengths and weaknesses in relation to other studies, discussing particularly any differences in results Although no studies to date have reported the effect of vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy and early infancy on asthma during childhood, placebo controlled clinical trials have shown efficacy of vitamin D supplementation in children with asthma. A meta-analysis, published in 2015 (28) , that included data from five clinical trials which used a vitamin D doses ranging from 500 to 2000 IU/day in children aged 5 to 18 years, showed a statistically significant reduction in asthma exacerbations (29) (30) (31) (32) (33) . One further trial published since this meta-analysis, which enrolled 130 individuals aged 10 to 50 years and randomly assigned them vitamin D or placebo, showed a significant improvement in lung function after 24 weeks of the intervention (34) . This was a post-hoc analysis performed on additional data, measurement of SPT and serum IgE responses to aeroallergens at age 18 months. We collected data specifically for this analysis and prior to study unblinding. Thus, we did not initiate these analyses following an inspection of already collected data, which is the principle methodological weakness of a post-hoc approach (35) .Meaning of the study: possible mechanisms and implications for clinicians or policymakers Based upon specific serum IgE measurement the proportion of children sensitised to mites (14%) was greater than for all other aeroallergens tests (all <5%). In children, reported rates of mite antigen sensitisation range from 5% to 28% with population based estimates from NZ being at the higher end of this range (36) (37) (38) . Internationally, mite antigen sensitisation is one of the most prevalent forms of aeroallergen sensitisation (38) . The differences between study groups were seen to the antigens of the house dust mites D. farinae and D. pteronyssinus, but not to those of the storage mites Blomia and Lepidoglyphus which, while major sensitising aeroallergen in some countries, are not in NZ (39, 40) . Sensitisation to mites is associated with an increased risk of childhood asthma as shown in cohort studies where asthma prevalence in children was measured at an older age than in this study, for example seven and 11 years (41, 42) . That no association was evident is likely a reflection of the younger age at which we estimated asthma prevalence and the difficulty in diagnosing asthma in the first two years of life.Author Manuscript This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved Our demonstration that vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy and infancy prevents HDM sensitisation is consistent with vitamin D having immune modulatory functions. Vitamin D promotes the induction of regulatory T-cells which in turn can prevent the pathological immune responses to antigens that lead to clinical allergic disease (8) . In animal models, exposure to adequate vitamin D prior to sensitisation prevents cellular airway responses to aeroallergens (43) .Unanswered questions and future research As rates of aeroallergen antigen sensitisation increase with increasing age, between-group differences may emerge for sensitisation to the other aeroallergens tested and in clinical manifestations of allergic disease (44) . Longer-term follow-up is also important to ensure that vitamin D supplementation does not place some individuals at increased risk of atopic sensitisation and allergic disease. In birth cohort studies from the United States (10) and Germany (45) , both high (≥100 nmol/L) and low (<50 nmol/L) cord blood 25(OH)D concentrations were associated with an increased risk of aeroallergen sensitisation at age five years (10) , and higher maternal 25(OH)D concentration during late pregnancy was associated with an increased risk of food allergen sensitisation at age two years (45) . Children who become atopic are at increased risk of asthma and of having more severe and persistent asthma (46, 47) . We do not know if the differences in mite sensitisation and primary care asthma visits that we observed to age 18 months, as a result of vitamin D supplementation during late pregnancy and early infancy, are a temporary phenomenon or one that has lifelong consequences in terms of an individual's risk of developing asthma. The diagnosis of asthma is very insecure at age 18 months. Follow-up to an age when the diagnosis of asthma can be made with greater precision is necessary to determine the true effect of pregnancy and early infancy interventions on asthma prevalence (48) . Our findings do indicate though the potential for daily supplemental vitamin D to prevent asthma in populations where both vitamin D deficiency and asthma are prevalent. Studies with more robust asthma case definitions need to follow these preliminary findings.
