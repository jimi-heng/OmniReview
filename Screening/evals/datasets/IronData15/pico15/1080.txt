**O0029**
**H. pylori**
**Furazolidone-based triple and quadruple eradication therapy for H. pylori infection**
**Presenting Author: NONGHUA LV**
**Additional Authors: YONG XIE, YE CHEN, JIANGBIN WANG, WENZHONG LIU, ZHENYU ZHANG, WEIHONG WANG, YIQI DU, JIANMING XU, GUIYING ZHANG, LIJUAN HUO, XUEHONG WANG, XIAOYAN LI, CHUNHUI LAN, YANQING LI, HONG WANG, GUOXIN ZHANG, ZHIFA LV**
**Corresponding Author: NONGHUA LV**
**Affiliations: Department of Gastroenterology, The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University; Endoscopic Center of Nanfang Hospital of Southern Medical University; Department of Gastroenterology, The China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University; The Affiliated Renji Hospital of Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Department of Gastroenterology, The First Hospital of Nanjing City; The First Hospital of Peking University; The Affiliated Changhai Hospital of Second Military Medical University; Department of Gastroenterology, The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University; Department of Gastroenterology, Xiangya Hospital of Central South University; Department of Gastroenterology, The First Affiliated Hospital of Shanxi Medical University; Department of Gastroenterology, The Affiliated Hospital of Qinghai University; Department of Gastroenterology, The First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University; Endoscopic Center of Daping Hospital of Third Military Medical University; Department of Gastroenterology, Qilu Hospital of Shandong University; Department of Gastroenterology, the No. 1 People’s Hospital of Guangzhou City; Department of Gastroenterology, The People’s Hospital of Jiangsu Province; Digestive Disease Institute, the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University, Nanchang, China**

**Objective:** Successful eradication of Helicobacter pylori has been associated with different drug therapies, furazolidone-based quadruple therapy might offer advantages in contrast to standard 1-week triple therapy. **Methods:** A total of 754 H. pylori positive patients, among them, duodenal ulcer in 376 cases, chronic gastritis in 378, were enrolled at 16 different hospitals in China, and were randomly assigned to four groups as follows: patients in Group 1 and 2 received esomeprazole, bismuth, amoxicillin and furazolidone twice daily for 10 and 7 days, respectively; patients in Group 3 and 4 received esomeprazole, amoxicillin and furazolidone twice daily for 10 and 7 days, respectively. **Results:** Overall, 689 patients, among them, duodenal ulcer in 347 cases, chronic gastritis in 342, completed the scheme and were re-assessed with the 13C-urea breath test. The intention-to-treat analysis of the H. pylori eradication rates in Groups 1, 2, 3 and 4, in total patients, were 86.56%, 83.60%, 82.36% and 77.60%, respectively, in Group 1 was significantly higher than that in Group 4 (P < 0.05); in duodenal ulcer patients, were 89.25%, 84.21%, 80.43% and 81.25%, respectively; in chronic gastritis patients, were 83.87%, 82.98%, 84.21% and 73.96%, respectively; no significant difference among four groups (P > 0.05). According to the per protocol analysis, the H. pylori eradication rates in Groups 1, 2, 3 and 4, in total patients, were 94.71%, 90.80%, 90.59% and 85.14% respectively, in Group 1 was significantly higher than that in Group 4 (P < 0.01); in duodenal ulcer patients, were 97.65%, 89.89%, 88.10% and 87.64%, respectively, in Group 1 was significantly higher than that in Group 2, 3, 4 (P < 0.05, 0.01); in chronic gastritis patients, were 91.76%, 91.76%, 93.02% and 82.56%, respectively; no significant difference among four groups (P > 0.05). **Conclusion:** 10-day quadruple furazolidone-based therapies achieve satisfactory H. pylori eradication rates.

**Key Words:** 1. Multi-center RCT; 2. Helicobacter pylori; 3. Furazolidone; 4. Eradication

**O0030**
**H. pylori**
**BMSCs favor the immunosuppressive T cells skewing in a helicobacter pylori model of gastric cancer**
**Presenting Author: XIAOHUA HOU**
**Additional Authors: HUAN MA, RONG LIN, ZHEN DING, WEINA SHI, WEI QIAN, JUN SONG**
**Corresponding Author: XIAOHUA HOU**
**Affiliations: No**

**Objective:** Bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) play an important role in Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)-induced gastric carcinogenesis. While the mechanism is not well understood, BMSCs have been shown to contribute to the immunosuppressive response found in a number of diseases. **Methods:** Gastritis was induced in BALB/c mice by three orogastric inoculation of mouse-adapted strain H. pylori TN2GF4, given at one-day intervals. BMSCs were subserously infused into the antrum area of mice with long-term helicobacter pylori infection. Inflammatory responses were analyzed by simulation of T cell activation and cytokine production in vitro and in vivo. The effect of BMSCs infusion on the helper T-cell balance was assessed. **Results:** At 28 day post-transplantation, BMSCs migrated from the subserosal to the mucosal layer of the stomach. The grafted BMSCs significantly stimulated systemic and local interleukin-10 (IL-10) -secreting T cell and regulatory T cell (Treg) functions. This observation was correlated with an increased percentage of CD4+IL-10+ cells and CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ cells in splenic mononuclear cells compared to H. pylori-infected mice not receiving BMSCs. Moreover, inhibitory cytokines IL-10 and transforming growth factor-β1 increased in the gastric tissue, while there was a decrease in inflammatory interferon-γ (IFN-γ). BMSC-transplanted mice also developed elevated IL-10/IFN-γ secreting and Treg/Th17 ratios. A co-culture system in the presence or absence of BMSCs was also established to evaluate the immune responses in vitro. An increase in IL-10-secreting T cells and Tregs, associated with increased expression of Gata-3 and FoxP3, and generation of IL-10 in the supernatant was observed. **Conclusion:** These findings demonstrate that transplantation of BMSCs into a chronic H. pylori-infected mouse model results in the generation of an immunosuppressive environment. The local and systemic immunosuppression mediated by BMSCs was likely contributed to an environment that is compatible with the development of H. pylori-induced gastric cancer.

**Key Words:** 1. BMSCs; 2. helicobacter pylori; 3. gastric cancer; 4. T cell
