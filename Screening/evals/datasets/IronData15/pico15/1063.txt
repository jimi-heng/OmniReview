13C-UREA BREATH-ID SYSTEM FOR THE REAL-TIME DIAGNOSIS OF HELICOBATER PYLORI INFECTION

R.M. Zagari, L.H. Eusebi*, S. Rabitti, F. Bazzoli

Department of Clinical Medicine, University of Bologna, Italy

Background and aim: 13C-Urea Breath Test (UBT) is highly accurate for H.pylori diagnosis. Limits are the need to collect and store breath samples, the need of trained personnel for the breath analysis and a delayed result. The continuous, rapid and real-time BreathID system (Ordiion, Israel) is a new method to perform UBT that may overcome such limits, but very few data are available on its diagnostic reliability. We evaluated the reliability of the BreathID system for both the initial diagnosis of H.pylori and after eradication treatment.

Material and methods: Patients were tested simultaneously by conventional UBT (C-UBT) and BreathID. According to the conventional method, two breath samples, before and 30 minutes after ingestion of 75 mg of 13C-urea and 4 g of citric acid, were collected and analysed by an isotope ratio mass spectrometer. Simultaneously, the BreathID system sampled in continuous the patient’s breath through a nasal cannula measuring the 13CO2/12CO2 ratio in expired air by a molecular correlation spectrometry and providing automatically the result in real-time. The result was expressed as DOB (delta 13CO2 over baseline) using the threshold of 5 DOB to define a positive result for both methods. We compared the DOB results of the BreathID system to those of the C-UBT measuring the agreement by the line of equality and Kappa statistic (K).

Results: A total of 256 patients, 110 for the initial diagnosis and 146 after eradication treatment, were enrolled. DOB values of BreathID and C-UBT fitted close to the line of equality (Fig 1). The results of BreathID agreed with the C-UBT in 250 out of 256 (97.6%) patients (k=0.95). The BreathID result was negative in 167 out of 172 (97.1%) patients negative at the C-UBT and was positive in 83 out of 84 patients (98.8%) positive at the C-UBT. The agreement between the results of the BreathID and C-UBT was very good both in the initial diagnosis (107/110 patients, 97.3%, K=0.94) and after eradication treatment (143/146 patients, 98%, K=0.95). The BreathID result was obtained after a mean time of 13.7 minutes (SD=3.59) in negative and 12.4 minutes (SD=3.62) in positive patients.

MOXIFLOXACIN CONTAINING TRIPLE THERAPY VS. MOXIFLOXACIN AND BISMUTH-CONTAINING QUADRUPLE THERAPY FOR FIRST-LINE TREATMENT OF HELICOBACTER PYLORI (H. PYLORI) INFECTION

A.F. Ciccaglione*, L. Cellini, L. Grossi, A.M. Di Tullio, M. Pagliaro, R. Tavani, S. Cocciolillo, L. Marzio

G D’Annunzio University of Chieti-Pescara - School of Gastroenterology - Digestive Physiopathology Ospedale Spirito Santo, Pescara, Italy

Background and aim: Moxifloxacin (MOXI) based triple therapy has been suggested as an alternative first-line therapy for H. pylori infection in geographical areas with clarithromycin resistance exceeds 30% of strains. The resistance rate of H. pylori to MOXI is increased in the last two years from 12% to 25% in patients never treated in our region, while resistance to clarithromycin is stable, ranging from 40% to 59% (unpublished results). In a recent trial, it has been shown that the addition of bismuth subcitrate to the standard first line therapy improves eradication rate despite antibiotic resistance (Lancet 2011;377: 905-913).

Aim: To compare triple therapy with pantoprazole 20 mg b.i.d., amoxicillin 1 g b.i.d and MOXI 400 mg b.i.d. for 10 days (PAM) vs quadruple therapy with pantoprazole 20 mg b.i.d., amoxicillin 1 g b.i.d., MOXI 400 mg b.i.d. and bismuth subcitrate 240 mg b.i.d. for 10 days (PAMB) for the first-line treatment of H. pylori infection.

Material and methods: Consecutive H. pylori positive patients never treated in their past for the infection were randomly treated with PAM or PAMB for 10 days. All patients were found positive at 13 C-Urea breath test (UBT) performed within ten days prior to the start of the study. Successful outcome was confirmed with an UBT performed 8 weeks after the end of treatment with a DOB value less of 5. Chi square analysis was used for statistical comparison.

Results: 57 patients were enrolled in the PAM group and 50 in the PAMB group. One patient in each group did not return to control. H. pylori eradication rate was statistically significantly higher in the PAMB group vs the PAM group either with the per protocol (PP) and intention to treat (ITT) analysis (PP: PAMB 93.8% (46/49), PAM 78.5 (44/56) P<0.02; ITT: PAMB 92% (46/50), PAM 77.1% (44/57), P<0.03). Both treatment were well tolerated with no reported side effects.

Conclusions: the eradication rate of MOXI containing triple regimen may be improved with the addition of bismuth subcitrate that therefore may be considered as a valuable adjuvant to triple therapy in those areas where H.pylori shows an high resistance to fluoroquinoles.
