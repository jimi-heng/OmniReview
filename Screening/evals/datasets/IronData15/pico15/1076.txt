SEQUENTIAL QUADRUPLE THERAPY VERSUS STANDARD TRIPLE THERAPY FOR HELICOBACTER PYLORI ERADICATION IN A SOUTHERN EUROPEAN COUNTRY
D. Branquinho, N. Almeida, A. Fernandes, A. Casela, J. Pina Cabral, C. Gregório, M. Donato, C. Calhau, J. Romaozinho and C. Sofia
Coimbra University Hospital, Coimbra, Portugal

Background: Empiric triple therapy for Helicobacter pylori eradication has shown disappointing results in Portugal. Quadruple regimens are now proposed as first-line therapy but its efficacy must be evaluated.

Aims: To compare the efficacy of standard triple and sequential quadruple therapies as first-line treatment options and determine factors associated with treatment failure.

Patients and Methods: This retrospective study included 250 Helicobacter pylori infected patients (female-57.2%; mean age-52.7 ± 15.1 years) that received triple therapy (PPI, amoxicillin 1 g, clarithromycin 500 mg bid, 14 days) and another 250 patients (female-59.2%; mean age-51.8 ± 15.7 years) treated with sequential therapy (PPI + amoxicillin 1 g bid for 5 days and PPI + clarithromycin 500 mg + metronidazole/tinidazole 500 mg bid/tid in the following 5 days). Eradication was tested using urea breath test. Several variables that could affect success rate were analyzed (age, indications for eradication, gender, smoking, compliance, PPI and antibiotics posology).

Results: There were no differences between groups in terms of gender, age, smoking habits, compliance and indications for treatment. The eradication rate obtained with sequential treatment was higher than with triple therapy (90.4%, CI 95%: 86.1–93.5% versus 65.6%, CI 95%: 59.6–71.5%, p < 0.0001). In the sequential therapy group, administering metronidazole tid was more effective than bid regimen (96.5% vs 53.8%, p = 0.002) and the same was noted for full-dose PPI versus half-dose (97.8% vs 41.7%, p = 0.002).

Conclusions: Due to unacceptable failure rates, triple therapy should be abandoned in Portugal in favor of quadruple regimens. Sequential therapy is effective in clinical practice, but full-dose PPI and metronidazole regimen must be adopted to achieve better outcomes.

COMPARISON OF THE EFFICACY OF 10 DAY-TRIPLE THERAPY-BASED, BISMUTH-CONTAINING QUADRUPLE THERAPY WITH TRIPLE THERAPY FOR THE FIRST LINE THERAPY OF HELICOBACTER PYLORI
B. S. Moon,* S. W. Yi,* H. C. Lim* and Y. C. Lee†
*Yongin Severance Hospital, Yonsei University, Seoul, Korea; †Severance Hospital, Yonsei University, Seoul, Korea

The eradication rate of proton pump inhibitor (PPI) based-triple therapy has been decreased due to increased antibiotic resistance in Korea. In recent studies of the first-line therapy for the successful Helicobacter pylori (HP) eradication, triple therapy-based, bismuth-containing quadruple therapy (TBQ) is tried. The aim of this study was to compare the efficacy of 10 day-TBQ therapy for HP eradication with triple therapy.

Methods: Between Sep. 2008 and May. 2015, 769 patients with HP infections were allocated to 2 groups. Group A: TBQ group : PPI bid, AMX 1000 mg bid, CLA 500 mg bid, and triptoassiumdicitrato-bismuthate 600 mg bid for 10 days. Group B : PPI based- triple therapy group : PPI bid, amoxicillin (AMX)1000 mg bid and CLA 500 mg bid for 7 days. After 4 weeks, the eradication of HP was assessed by urea breath test, and the side effects were assessed by questionnaire.

Results: The eradication rate of TBQ therapy was higher than triple therapy in intention to treat analysis 87.6% (319/364) vs 66.8% (267/400) (p < 0.001) and per protocol analysis 87.6% (319/364) vs 67.6% (267/395)) (p < 0.001). The side effects were dark stool (8.9%), taste alteration (8.9%), abdominal pain (6.8%) in TBQ group and the withdrawal due to side effects was no significant differences between two groups (6/369 vs 5/400).

Conclusion: TBQ therapy for 10 days was more effective than triple therapy to eradicate HP as first line therapy with mild and moderate side effects in Korea

FOURTEEN- VERSUS SEVEN-DAY BISMUTH-BASED QUADRUPLE THERAPY FOR SECOND-LINE HELICOBACTER PYLORI ERADICATION
J. Hwang, D. Lee, H. Yoon, C. Shin, Y. Park and N. Kim
Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, Seongnam, Korea

Aim: To compare the efficacy of 14- and 7-day bismuth-based quadruple therapies as second-line eradication treatment for Helicobacter pylori infection.

Methods: Between 2004 and 2014, the medical records of 790 patients who had experienced failure of first-line proton pump inhibitor (PPI)-based eradication therapy and were then treated with bismuth-based quadruple therapy were retrospectively reviewed. Those who received bismuth-based quadruple therapy (PPI, bismuth, metronidazole, and tetracycline; PBMT) for either 7 days or 14 days were assigned to a PBMT-7 group (n = 543) or a PBMT-14 group (n = 247), respectively.

Results: Final ITT eradication rates were 67.4% (366/543; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 63.1–71.7%) in the PBMT-7 group and 72.8% (180/247; 95% CI: 67.4–78.2%) in the PBMT-14 group (p = 0.028). The final PP eradication rates were 78.2% (366/468; 95% CI: 72.1–84.0%) in the PBMT-7 group and 84.1% (180/214; 95% CI: 76.8–90.8%) in the PBMT-14 group (p = 0.009). The H. pylori eradication rates in the PBMT-14 group were significantly higher than in the PBMT-7 group according to both ITT (p = 0.028) and PP analysis (p = 0.009). Compliance was similar in both groups (PBMT-7 group: 97.9%; PBMT-14 group: 96.4%). Adverse event rates were 10.7% (51/478) and 17.1% (38/222) in the PBMT-7 and PBMT-14 groups, respectively (p = 0.487).

Conclusion: The 14-day bismuth-based quadruple therapy is a significantly more effective second-line eradication treatment for H. pylori infection than the 7-day alternative.

EFFICACY AND TOLERABILITY OF HYBRID THERAPY FOR HELICOBACTER PYLORI INFECTION: PRELIMINARY RESULTS OF A PROSPECTIVE TRIAL IN GREECE
S. D. Georgopoulos,* V. Papastergiou,† E. Xirouchakis,* I. Familias† and S. Karatapanis†
*Athens Medical Paleon Faliron General Hospital, Paleon Phaliron, Greece; †General Hospital of Rhodes, Rhodes, Greece

Introduction: Increasing antibiotic resistance rates have challenged the efficacy of regimens to eradicate Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection.

Aim: To examine the efficacy and tolerability of a 14-day, quadruple, nonbismuth-containing hybrid regimen.

Patients and methods: Prospective, dual-center, open-label study including treatment-naïve patients diagnosed with H. pylori infection by positive rapid urease test and/or histology. All patients received a 14-day regimen consisted of a 7-day dual therapy (40 mg esomeprazole and 1 gr amoxicillin, bid) followed by a 7-day quadruple therapy (40 mg esomeprazole, 1 gr amoxicillin, 500 mg clarithromycin and 500 mg metronidazole, all bid). Eradication was defined by negative ¹³C-urea breath test or histology, 4–6 weeks after treatment completion.

Results: Seventy patients (mean age: 52.5 years, 46 males, 31.4% smokers 24.3% peptic ulcers) were included. The eradication rates were 60/70 (85%, 95% CI: 78.5–90) by intention-to-treat (ITT) and 60/67 (89.5%, 95% CI: 82.3–92.1) by per-protocol analysis. Adverse events were reported by 21 (30%) patients; drug compliance was excellent with 95.7% of the patients taking >90% of the prescribed medication.

Conclusions: Hybrid therapy seems to offer optimal tolerability and acceptable eradication efficacy (ITT close to 90%). Further data are warranted to validate hybrid therapy as first-line regimen for H. pylori eradication in Greece.
