**OE-0783 (PE-0299) Efficacy of Helicobacter pylori eradication with vonoprazan-based triple therapy**

**Authors: HIROTO NODA; OSAMU GOTO; ERIKO KOIZUMI; SHUHEI AGAWA; GO IKEDA; KAZUTOSHI HIGUCHI; TEPPEI AKIMOTO; HIROSHI YAMAWAKI; YASUHIRO KODAKA; NOBUE UEKI; SEIJI FUTAGAMI; SHUNJI FUJIMORI; MITSURU KAISE; KATSUHIKO IWAKIRI**

**Affiliation:** Department of Gastroenterology, Division of Gastroenterology, Nippon Medical School, Bunkyo-Ku, Japan

**Background and Aim:** Vonoprazan is a K competitive channel inhibitor, which potently inhibits gastric acid secretion. Efficiency of vonoprazan is expected in Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) eradication. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of H. pylori eradication with vonoprazan-based triple therapy compared with the conventional therapy.

**Methods:** We retrospectively investigated H. pylori eradications at Nippon Medical School, Chiba Hokusoh Hospital. The H. pylori eradications were used to treat patients with a history of H. pylori-infected gastritis and gastric or duodenal ulcers caused by H. pylori. The acid secretion inhibitor used for eradication was arbitrarily selected by an outpatient clinician. We compared the success rates of primary and secondary eradication using vonoprazan with those of the conventional therapy in the same period. Primary eradication regimen was amoxicillin (AMPC), clarithromycin (CAM), and vonoprazan or PPI. In secondary eradication, CAM was changed to metronidazole (MNZ). The cases of secondary eradication included cases in which primary eradication was performed at other hospitals.

**Results:** We analyzed 334 cases during the study period. The success rates of primary H. pylori eradication were 92.5% (success 187 cases, unsuccessful 15 cases, cessation 3 cases) with vonoprazan-based triple therapy versus 82.5% (success 85 cases, unsuccessful 18 cases, cessation 2 cases) with PPI therapy. The main reasons for cessation were vomiting, diarrhea, and fever. When analyzed except for the cases of cessation, the primary eradication rate was significantly higher in the vonoprazan-based triple therapy ($p = 0.011$). The success rates of secondary H. pylori eradication were 90.9% (success 30 cases, unsuccessful 3 cases) with vonoprazan-based triple therapy versus 93.8% (success 15 cases, unsuccessful 1 case) with PPI therapy.

**Conclusion:** Helicobacter pylori eradication with vonoprazan-based triple therapy was superior to the conventional therapy in primary eradication.

**Keywords:** vonoprazan, H. pylori

**OE-0838 (PE-0300) TL quadruple therapy is superior to AL quadruple therapy and AL triple therapy in the rescue treatment of H. pylori infection**

**Authors: PING-I HSU[1]; FENG-WOEI TSAI[1]; DENG-CHYANG WU[2]**

**Affiliation:** [1]Department of Gastroenterology and Hepatology, Kaohsiung Veterans General Hospital, and [2]Department of Internal Medicine, Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital, Kaohsiung, Taiwan

**Background and Aim:** Fluoroquinolone triple therapy is a recommended second-line treatment for Helicobacter pylori infection in the Maastricht V/Florence Consensus Report. However, the eradication rate of this salvage treatment is suboptimal. The study aimed to compare the efficacy of tetracycline–levofloxacin quadruple therapy (TL quadruple therapy), amoxicillin–levofloxacin quadruple therapy (AL quadruple therapy), and amoxicillin–levofloxacin triple therapy (AL triple therapy) in the rescue treatment of H. pylori infection.

**Methods:** From January 2014 to May 2018, 203 H. pylori-infected patients with failure of first-line treatment were treated by a TL quadruple therapy (esomeprazole 40 mg b.d., bismuth 120 mg q.d.s., tetracycline 500 mg q.d.s., and levofloxacin 500 mg o.d.; $n = 86$), AL quadruple therapy (esomeprazole 40 mg b.d., bismuth 120 mg q.d.s., amoxicillin 500 mg q.d.s., and levofloxacin 500 mg o.d.; $n = 29$), or AL triple therapy (esomeprazole 40 mg b.d., amoxicillin 500 mg q.d.s., and levofloxacin 500 mg o.d.; $n = 88$ for 10 days. They were followed up by a standard protocol, and H. pylori status was assessed by urea breath test 6 weeks after treatment.

**Results:** Intention-to-treat analysis showed that TL quadruple therapy achieved a markedly higher eradication rate than AL quadruple therapy and AL triple therapy (97.7% vs 69.0% and 70.9%; both $P < 0.001$). The three treatment groups had comparable drug adherence (90.9%, 89.7%, and 97.7%, respectively), but TL and AL quadruple therapies exhibited higher frequencies of adverse events than AL triple therapy (29.5% and 31.0% vs 15.1%). Further analysis revealed that the three therapies had comparable eradication rates for levofloxacin-sensitive H. pylori strains but TL quadruple therapy achieved a higher eradication rate than AL triple therapy for levofloxacin-resistant strains (100% vs 50.0%; $P = 0.025$).

**Conclusion:** TL quadruple therapy is superior to AL quadruple therapy and AL triple therapy in the rescue treatment of H. pylori infection.

**Keywords:** Helicobacter pylori, second-line, treatment, antibiotic resistance
