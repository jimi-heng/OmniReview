Comparison of 10-day levofloxacin bismuth-based quadruple therapy and levofloxacin-based triple therapy for Helicobacter pylori

Division of *Gastroenterology, Department of Internal Medicine, ‡Infectious Diseases, Department of Internal Medicine, Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital, §Division of Gastroenterology, Department of Internal Medicine, Kaohsiung Veterans’ General Hospital and National Yangming University, §Faculty of Medicine, Department of Medicine, College of Medicine, ¶Center for Stem Cell Research, Kaohsiung Medical University, **Department of Internal Medicine, Kaohsiung Municipal Tatung Hospital, ‡§School of Medicine, College of Medicine, E-Da Hospital, I-Shou University, Kaohsiung, §Department of Internal Medicine, Pingtung Hospital, Ministry of Health and Welfare, Pingtung, and ††Department of Gastroenterology, Changhua Christian Hospital, Changhua, Taiwan, China

OBJECTIVE: This was a prospective study aiming to investigate whether levofloxacin plus bismuth-based quadruple therapy was more effective than levofloxacin-based triple therapy after failed first-line eradication therapies for Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection.

METHODS: Sixty-seven patients infected with H. pylori were randomly assigned to two groups; the levofloxacin plus bismuth-based quadruple therapy group (RBAL [n = 33]; rabeprazole 20 mg twice daily, bismuth subcitrate 120 mg four times daily, amoxicillin 1 g twice daily and levofloxacin 500 mg once daily, for 10 days) and the levofloxacin-based triple therapy group (RAL [n = 34]; rabeprazole 20 mg twice daily, amoxicillin 1 g twice daily and levofloxacin 500 mg once daily, for 10 days). Endoscopy was performed 4–8 weeks after H. pylori eradication to assess treatment response. We followed up patient response and compliance and checked their resistance to antibiotics.

RESULTS: Intention-to-treat analysis revealed that both groups had similar eradication rates (RBAL vs RAL: 84.8% [95% confidence interval {CI} 72.6–97.1%] vs 67.6% [95% CI 51.9–83.4%], P = 0.0987). No significant differences in compliance or adverse events were found (P = 0.9829 and 0.0720). Episolemeter test showed that most eradication failure cases were levofloxacin-resistant.

CONCLUSIONS: Adding bismuth subcitrate to levofloxacin-based triple therapy was not more effective than not doing so, but no further side effects were noted. Both eradication therapies were equally safe and patients had the same tolerance to both regimens. Resistance rate to levofloxacin may be important when choosing second-line therapy.

KEY WORDS: bismuth, Helicobacter pylori, levofloxacin, quadruple therapy, triple therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) is a Gram-negative bacterium often found around the pylorus ring, which

---

# Page 2

contributes to many gastrointestinal (GI) diseases such as peptic ulcer, gastric cancer, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma and dyspepsia. The eradication of H. pylori is an important step in the treatment of these diseases, and triple therapy is no longer the gold standard for first-line therapy due to the increasing rate of H. pylori resistance to clarithromycin. According to the recommendations of the Maastricht IV Consensus Report, bismuth-containing quadruple therapy should be used as the first-line therapy in regions of high clarithromycin resistance.

Quinolones have been suggested as an alternative first-line therapy or second-line rescue therapy. A meta-analysis conducted in 2006 found that when treating patients who were persistently infected with H. pylori, 10-day levofloxacin-based triple therapy was more effective than 7-day bismuth-based quadruple therapy and patients had better tolerance to this regimen. But for clarithromycin-resistant strains, especially in patients who had experienced treatment failure, the incidence of quinolone resistance was high.

The Maastricht IV Consensus recommends bismuth-containing quadruple therapy as a second-line therapy when standard triple therapy or non-bismuth-containing quadruple first-line therapy fails. Levofloxacin and amoxicillin triple therapy is used when bismuth-containing quadruple first-line therapy fails. H. pylori culture is not performed and drug resistance is not checked at the first-line eradication therapy, even at the second-line eradication therapy, in most areas. We added bismuth to levofloxacin and amoxicillin triple therapy in the hope of increasing the second-line eradication rate.

This study was undertaken to determine whether the addition of bismuth to levofloxacin-based triple therapy was effective as a second-line therapy for treating H. pylori infection.

**MATERIALS AND METHODS**

**Patients**

We enrolled patients visiting the Gastroenterological Clinics of Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital and Kaohsiung Veterans’ General Hospital between August 2012 and December 2013 with dyspepsia who failed the standard triple first-line H. pylori eradication therapy (proton pump inhibitor [PPI] twice daily, 500 mg clarithromycin twice daily and 1 g amoxicillin twice daily). All patients had mucosal biopsy, esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) or ¹³C-urea breath test (UBT) to check if they were infected by H. pylori. Patients were excluded if: (i) they had taken antibiotics and PPI within the previous 4 weeks; (ii) had previous gastric surgery; (iii) had severe comorbidities; (iv) were allergic to amoxicillin, levofloxacin or bismuth; or (v) were pregnant.

Due to the health insurance payments in Taiwan, peptic ulcer confirmed by EGD is the main criterion for receiving first-line eradication therapy for H. pylori infection.

The study was approved by the Medical Committee of the Kaohsiung Veterans’ General Hospital and Kaohsiung Medical University.

**H. pylori eradication therapy**

All patients who had failed standard triple therapy were randomly assigned to two groups. The first group (RBAL) received levofloxacin plus bismuth-based quadruple therapy (rabeprazole 20 mg twice daily, bismuth subcitrate 120 mg four times daily, amoxicillin 1 g twice daily and levofloxacin 500 mg once daily, for 10 days). The other group (RAL) was prescribed levofloxacin-based triple therapy (rabeprazole 20 mg twice daily, amoxicillin 1 g twice daily and levofloxacin 500 mg once daily, for 10 days). Patient compliance and adverse events were recorded when the patient returned to the hospital. H. pylori infection status was confirmed in patients receiving repeated EGD examination 16 weeks after the treatment via mucosal biopsy for rapid urease test (RUT), histology and H. pylori culture. Patients who refused endoscopic survey were given the UBT. PPIs were discontinued 2 weeks before these procedures.

**Diagnosis of H. pylori infection**

**RUT**

Gastric mucosal specimens obtained by EGD were put on a RUT gel (Delta West, Perth, Western Australia, Australia) for 6 h at room temperature. The result was considered positive when the color changed from yellowish to pinkish.

**UBT**

Patients were asked to keep nothing by mouth (NPO) for at least 4 h, and breathe out through a straw into the test tube as a baseline sample. They were then asked to drink the urea solution (99% ¹³C-urea 100 mg [Princeton International Industries Taiwan Corporation, Taiwan, China] in 50 mL sterile water). A second breathing sample was then collected after 25

min. All the samples were sent for analysis by using a continuous-flow isotope ratio mass spectrometer (CFIRMS; Europa Scientific Ltd., Crewe, UK). Values were expressed as an excess $\text{^13CO}_2$ excretion per mil units: $\text{^13CO}_2 = (\text{Rsamp-Rstd})/\text{Rstd} \times 1000$. Rsamp and Rstd represent the ratios of $\text{^13C}$ to $\text{^12C}$ in the test and baseline samples. The cut-off value was 4 per mil at 20 min after drinking $\text{^13C}$-urea.

**H. pylori culture and antimicrobial resistance**

Specimens were obtained from antral gastric biopsy for the isolation of $H. pylori$, as described previously. Antibiotic susceptibility was tested by E test (bioMérieux Diagnostics, Lyon, France) in patients with positive $H. pylori$ culture. $H. pylori$ strains with a maximal inhibitory concentration values of 4, >1, >8, >1 and >0.5 mg/L were considered to be resistant to tetracycline, levofloxacin, metronidazole, clarithromycin and amoxicillin, respectively.

**Analysis of cytochrome P450 2C19 (CYP2C19) genotypes**

Peripheral blood sample was obtained from the patients before the eradication therapy and after they provided their written informed consent. The sample was then centrifugated to collect the leukocytes. DNA was extracted from the leukocytes by using a commercially available kit (QIAGEN K.K., Tokyo, Japan) and was stored until analysis.

**Questionnaire**

The questionnaire included the patients’ personal history, including their medical history, use of pain killers, intake of coffee or tea, smoking history and compliance. Adverse events included diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain, anorexia, nausea or vomiting, skin rash, headache, dizziness, parageusia, and fatigue, etc.

**Outcomes**

The end-point of this study was the successful or unsuccessful eradication of $H. pylori$.

**Statistical analysis**

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 19.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, whereas categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages. The $\chi^2$ test was used to compare demographic distributions, efficacy and side effects. Intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) analyses were used to evaluate the eradication rate of $H. pylori$. All randomized patients who had ever received eradication therapy were included in the ITT analysis. When patients’ $H. pylori$ infection status was not confirmed they were included as having failed $H. pylori$ eradication in the ITT analysis. In the PP analysis, patients whose $H. pylori$ infectious status was not confirmed were included in the PP analysis.

RESULTS

A total of 73 patients were included, of whom six (two in the RBAL group and four in the RAL group) did not take any drugs and were lost to follow-up (Fig. 1). Finally, the RAL and the RBAL groups consisted of 34 and 33 patients, respectively. The RAL group had an average age of \( 56.8 \pm 10.2 \) years; among them, 12 patients were males and 4 were smokers. Among the 33 patients at a mean age of \( 50.6 \pm 11.9 \) years in the RBAL group, 10 were males, and three of them were smokers. There were no differences in the characteristics between the two groups (Table 1).

The eradication rate of the RBAL group was 84.8% (28/33) by ITT analysis and 84.4% (27/32) by PP analysis, while that of the RAL group was 67.6% (23/34) by ITT analysis and 66.7% (22/33) by PP analysis. The eradication rate of the RBAL group was higher than that of the RAL group, by both ITT and PP analysis, although the differences were not statistically significant (\( P = 0.0987 \) and 0.0975, respectively; Table 2). The compliance between the two groups was not different (97.1% vs 97.0%, \( P = 0.9829 \)).

In all, \( H. pylori \) strain in 10 patients was resistant to metronidazole, and their eradication rate was 60.0%. Only five patients had \( H. pylori \) resistance to levofloxacin, but \( H. pylori \) eradication failed in all these patients. Seven patients had \( H. pylori \) resistance to clarithromycin, and their eradication rate was 57.1% (Table 3). In patients whose CYP2C19 genotypes were homozygous extensive metabolizer (Hom-EM), the eradication rate was 69.6%, and the risk ratio (RR) was 1.75 (95% CI 0.519–5.895, \( P = 0.372 \)). We also analyzed other possible clinical factors influencing the efficacy of \( H. pylori \) eradication therapy (Table 3).

The RAL group reported no side effects while the RBAL group had side effects such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, headache, dizziness, taste change and fatigue, but there was no statistical significance, although the side effects of the RBAL group did not influence patient compliance (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

About a decade ago, \( H. pylori \) resistance rate to levofloxacin in Taiwan was less than 10%;[11,12] therefore, a second-line or rescue therapy containing levofloxacin was used[6,13–16]. Nevertheless, some reports have shown that the efficacy of levofloxacin-based second-line therapy was not as good as that of the RBAL group.[17,18] The reasons for this may be that the RBAL group was not as effective as the RAL group in eradicating \( H. pylori \).

line therapy seems to be decreasing. We found that the *H. pylori* eradication rate using RAL therapy was 67.6% (ITT analysis), which is lower than that reported in other studies. In 2016 two studies showed eradication rates of levofloxacin-based triple therapy of 75.3% and 80.5%. Any therapy that has an eradication rate of less than 70% is not acceptable, this was also the reason that we did not continue this study. One report showed that levofloxacin resistance in Taiwan from 2010 to 2015 was as high as 37.5%. Levofloxacin-based triple therapy should be used with caution, and the increasing resistance rate should be closely monitored.

In our study, the rate of *H. pylori* culture was low, but levofloxacin resistance seemed to play an important role; in patients with resistance to levofloxacin, the eradication of *H. pylori* failed regardless of RAL or RBAL therapy. With an increasing levofloxacin resistance, the efficacy of levofloxacin-based therapy has been decreasing gradually. A study in Japan showed that clarithromycin-resistant strains of *H. pylori* also had high rates of quinolone resistance, particularly in patients with previous eradication failure. Standard triple therapy is still used in some areas of Taiwan and clarithromycin resistance is an important issue.

When choosing a second-line therapy for *H. pylori* infection, levofloxacin-based triple therapy should be administered very carefully. Compared with these two kinds of therapies, changing clarithromycin to levofloxacin, while keeping the other medication the same, may be the reason for the low eradication rate of RAL. Although adding bismuth to levofloxacin-based therapy did not seem to be more efficacious, it had a higher *H. pylori* eradication rate (84.8% by ITT analysis). The addition of bismuth to levofloxacin-based therapy may be a choice when levofloxacin-containing regimens are considered.

*H. pylori* eradication therapy may cause the bacterium to migrate to the stomach, which may affect the result of endoscopic mucosal biopsy. We tried to decrease this situation by taking biopsy from the gastric body and antrum, but still could not avoid it completely. Patients in our study were given EGD examination with mucosal biopsy or C-UBT to confirm the eradication rate because some patients refused to have a second endoscopic examination. This was a limitation of our study.

CYP2C19 genotypes may influence the metabolism of various PPIs, and the use of high-dose or low-dose

PPI may be related to the efficacy of bismethyl containing therapies. We chose rabeprazole 20 mg twice daily, because rabeprazole is not affected by CYP2C19 genetics. We also analyzed the effect of CYP2C19 polymorphism, and found that 23 patients had the Hom-EM CYP2C19 genotype, but without obvious statistical significance (Table 3). The choice of PPI may diminish the impact of CYP2C19 genotypes. Interaction between PPIs and clopidogrel has recently been a hot issue. CYP2C19 is an enzyme that metabolizes PPIs, but clopidogrel needs CYP2C19 to become active. In patients needing clopidogrel, the choice of PPIs is important. But new types of anti-coagulation agents, like dabigatran, rivaroxaban and edoxaban, do not interact with PPIs. The choice of PPIs and anti-coagulation agents may be more efficacious and safe by using these new drugs.

The low culture rate of H. pylori was the major limitation of our study. We are unable to discuss the relationship between clarithromycin and levofloxacin resistance as more detailed data was needed. Resistance to both antibiotics seems to be increasing in Taiwan, and this will influence the eradication of H. pylori infection. When high clarithromycin resistance is the focus, levofloxacin-based triple therapy as second-line eradication may not be the best first choice.
