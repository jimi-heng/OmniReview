Triple or Quadruple Tetracycline-Based Therapies Versus Standard Triple Treatment for Helicobacter pylori Treatment

Yildiran Songür, MD, Altug Şenol, MD, Ayşe Balkarlı, MD, Abdülkadir Baştürk, MD and Süreyya Çerçi, MD

Abstract: Background: Our aim was to compare lansoprazole-tetracycline-metranidazole (LTM) as first-line treatment with the classical lansoprazole-amoxicillin-clarithromycin (LAC) and bismuth-containing quadruple treatments. Patients and Methods: This prospective, single-center, randomized study included 464 consecutive Helicobacter pylori-positive patients with dyspeptic symptoms. A total of 415 patients completed the study. The patients were allocated into 4 study groups using random sampling numbers as follows—LAC group: lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily, amoxicillin 1000 mg twice daily, and clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily for 14 days; BLTM group: bismuth subcitrate 300 mg 4 times a day, lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily, tetracycline 500 mg 4 times a day, and metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 10 days; RBLTM group: ranitidine bismuth citrate 400 mg twice daily, lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily, tetracycline 500 mg 4 times a day, and metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 10 days; and LTM group: lansoprazole 30 mg twice daily, tetracycline 500 mg 4 times a day, and metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 10 days. Results: The per protocol H. pylori eradication rate in LAC, BLTM, RBLTM, and LTM groups were 37 of 104 (35.6%), 56 of 102 (54.9%), 67 of 104 (64.4%), and 63 of 105 (60%), respectively. The intention-to-treat eradication rate was 37 of 113 (32.7%) in LAC, 56 of 119 (47.1%) in BLTM, 67 of 117 (57.3%) in RBLTM, and 63 of 115 (54.8%) in LTM group. The BLTM, RBLTM, and LTM treatment groups achieved a significantly better eradication rate than the LAC treatment group (P < 0.001). There was not any significant statistical difference between the groups of BLTM, RBLTM, and LTM. Conclusion: LTM treatment group achieved a significantly better eradication rate than the LAC treatment group. The success ratio of LTM therapy is comparable with quadruple bismuth-based treatments.

Key Indexing Terms: Helicobacter pylori; Eradication; Tetracycline; Therapy; Bismuth. [Am J Med Sci 2009;338(1):50–53.]

Despite the current availability of highly effective therapeutic regimens, treatment of Helicobacter pylori has not been totally successful. Fifty percent of the population in developed countries and 80% to 90% of the population in developing countries are estimated to carry this pathogen. Turkey has a high rate of H. pylori prevalence, reported as approximately 80%. Although the treatment of H. pylori infection is relatively successful in developed countries, with eradication of the organism, usually in excess of 80% of patients, this ratio is lower in developing countries.

The most successful and universal treatment is triple therapy, with 3 drugs twice a day [proton pump inhibitors (PPI), amoxicillin, and clarithromycin (CLA)], but recently, especially because of increasing CLA resistance, the success ratio with this treatment has decreased in our country. Studies conducted in Turkey in 2004–2005 showed a rate of resistance to CLA of 20% to 24%, but Onder et al reported this ratio as 48.2%. In fact, possibly also because of this increased CLA resistance, Altintas et al reported that the H. pylori eradication rate with a standard triple therapy (PPI plus amoxicillin and CLA) for 14 days was 45%.

In developing countries, because of the low success ratios seen with the classical triple treatment, bismuth-containing quadruple treatments are being applied as first-line or second-line therapies. However, difficulties with patient compliance, the high rate of discarding medicine compared with other treatments, and lack of availability in all countries are the disadvantages. PPI-amoxicillin or tetracycline and metronidazole are recommended if bismuth is not available. If the eradication rate obtained with PPI-tetracycline-metronidazole treatment is at an acceptable level, it facilitates better patient compliance and avoids the need for bismuth. Interestingly, even though it is recommended, there are very little data in the literature regarding triple treatment like PPI-tetracycline-metronidazole. Our aim was to compare lansoprazole-tetracycline-metranidazole (LTM) as first-line treatment with the classical lansoprazole-amoxicillin-CLA (LAC) and treatments with bismuth-containing quadruple treatments.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

Our study included all consecutive H. pylori-positive patients with dyspeptic symptoms treated in our hospital in 2005–2007. The study protocol was approved by the local ethics committee. This was a prospective, single-blind, randomized study performed in 1 center. The presence of H. pylori was assessed in patients by urea breath test (UBT) (70%) or histologic examination of gastric biopsy obtained at endoscopy (30%). Those who had previously received H. pylori eradication therapy and those who were treated with nonsteroidal antiinflammatory drugs, PPI, H2 receptor blockers, or antimicrobials within the previous 4 weeks were excluded. Other exclusion criteria were pregnancy, history of allergy to penicillin or any antibiotic, and previous gastric surgery.

Patient compliance with medication was evaluated at the end of treatment by pill count, and was considered as good if more than 80% of the medication were consumed. Side effects were also documented by a direct questioning approach by an investigator who was not aware of the assigned treatment of the patients. UBT was performed by an investigator who was blind to the assigned treatment of the patient 6 weeks after completion of the therapy. Successful eradication of bacteria was defined as a negative UBT.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analysis was performed according to intention-to-treat and per protocol (PP) principles. The 95% confidence interval was determined for all eradication rates. $\chi^2$ Test was used to determine the difference in eradication between the groups. Statistical Package for Social Sciences (version 15.0) programs was used.

RESULTS

From 2005 to 2007, a total of 464 patients were screened for the study. Forty-nine patients dropped out because of side effects or failed to return for the follow-up test (Table 1). A total of 415 patients completed the study [257 women (61.9%), 158 men (38.1%)]. The patients were aged between 18 and 83 (mean: 43.56 ± 13.51) years. One hundred four patients were assigned into the LAC group; 102 into BLTM; 104 into RBLTM; and 105 into LTM. Overall, compliance rates with LAC, BLTM, RBLTM, and LTM therapies were 91%, 87%, 90%, and 94%, respectively.

The demographic characters of the patients who were enclosed into the different treatment groups were homogeneous, and there was not any significant difference between the groups with regard to age, sex, tobacco use, and endoscopy findings ($P > 0.05$) (Table 2).

The intention-to-treat and per protocol $H$. pylori eradication rates are given in Table 3. The BLTM, RBLTM, and LTM treatment groups achieved a significantly better eradication rate than the LAC treatment group ($P < 0.001$). There was no statistically significant difference between the LTM treatment group and the bismuth-based treatments with respect to eradication success. Drugs were usually well tolerated in most of the patients. Distribution of side effects according to treatment groups is shown in Table 4.

DISCUSSION

Factors that contribute to the failure of anti-$H$. pylori therapy include those related to the choice of the formulated regimen, antimicrobial resistance (especially to CLA and imidazole compounds), treatment compliance, geographical factors, dose of the PPI used, and the gastroduodenal disorder to be treated. Recently, host-related factors, such as the presence of polymorphisms in cytochrome P450 and isoenzyme 2C19, which is responsible for PPI metabolization, and of interleukin-1B–related polymorphisms were reported as the determining factors in therapeutic success.

At present, no study in the English literature has evaluated a 10-day regimen using PPI in combination with metronidazole and tetracycline. This is the first study to compare the eradication rates of bismuth-based quadruple therapy and LTM. The results of the current study showed that the $H$. pylori eradication rate with a standard triple therapy (PPI plus amoxicillin and CLA) for 14 days was 35.6% in Turkey. When the LAC treatment group was compared with the other regimen groups regarding success, ratios were significantly higher than observed with LAC ($P < 0.001$, $\chi^2$ test). These results are in accordance with a recent study from the same area, which has

shown that the BLTM treatment achieved a significantly better eradication rate compared with LAC treatment (57% versus 70%) in Turkish patients. Mantzaris et al. obtained better eradication rates with triple therapy than with quadruple therapy for *H. pylori* infection in patients with duodenal ulcer. However, the differences did not reach statistical significance. Similar results have been reported in a meta-analysis of 4 selected trials in which triple and quadruple therapies was found to be roughly equivalent in terms of effectiveness, compliance, and side-effects profile when administered as first-line treatment for *H. pylori* infection. However, the majority of the patients in these studies were treated for 7 days, whereas the patients in our study were treated for 10 to 14 days. More patients on LBMT and RBLMT were withdrawn from the study because of treatment-related side effects in our study. There was no statistically significant difference in the success ratios between LTM and BLTM and RBLTM (*P* > 0.05, *χ*² test). We showed good tolerance of the LTM triple therapy compared with the bismuth-based quadruple therapy. However, it should be noted that the eradication rates achieved by all these treatments are remarkably low. Because all schemes did not reach an eradication rate of 80%, which is conventionally considered as the limit for the real effectiveness of a treatment, they are not proposed in the clinical practice.

When the situation in Turkey is considered, it is seen that the ratio of success for 14-day treatments is higher than with 7-day and 10-day treatments. Thus, it seems that duration affects the success ratio. However, continuation of quadruple therapies for 14 days is quite difficult with respect to tolerability. Because LTM treatment is better tolerated by the patients, a 14-day LTM regimen seems to be a reasonable treatment, and should be investigated in further studies. The relatively higher ratio observed with this regimen, despite metronidazole resistance, might indicate that CLA resistance is more important. In fact, it is quite possible to overcome metronidazole resistance over 7-day periods.

In conclusion, in our population, triple LTM and quadruple bismuth-based treatment groups achieved a significantly better eradication rate than LAC treatment group, which demonstrated a low success ratio of 35%. However, all therapeutic schemes used in this study were not satisfactorily effective to be proposed in the first-line treatment. Therefore, new regimens are necessary to achieve a better *H. pylori* cure rate.
